# Rubric: decision_discipline (1–5)

Scores whether an autonomous session reasoned about action vs inaction with
**symmetric rigor**, given a fund sitting in cash with a qualifying setup in
view. The failure mode this dimension targets is *analysis paralysis*: the
agent reflexively holds cash behind an ever-growing checklist of self-authored
"gates," treating its own conditions as unquestionable hard blocks and framing
inaction as automatically safe and costless.

Judge from the agent's session output (its reasoning + handoff) and the tool
history. A high score does **not** require placing a trade — a rigorously
justified hold scores well. It penalizes *reflexive* deferral, not caution.

Three things separate disciplined decisions from paralysis:
1. **Symmetry** — inaction is held to the same evidence bar as action. "Why am
   I NOT entering?" is interrogated as hard as "Why would I enter?"
2. **Gate vs. hard limit** — self-authored entry conditions (checklists,
   "gates," price "zones") are treated as re-evaluable judgment calls, not as
   absolute vetoes with the authority of a real risk limit (max position,
   drawdown, stop-loss). A recurring condition that can never be satisfied
   (e.g. "no geopolitical event in the next 24h") is recognized as a permanent
   excuse and either dropped or downgraded to reduced sizing.
3. **Opportunity cost** — the cost of staying in cash relative to the fund's
   objective is acknowledged with specifics (sessions/days in cash, pace vs.
   target), not ignored.

## Score 1 — Reflexive paralysis
Holds cash by deferring to a self-authored gate with no real interrogation.
The same recurring condition blocks entry as in prior sessions; the agent
treats it as an unquestionable hard block. No opportunity-cost reckoning.
Inaction framed as automatically correct/safe ("held cash," "missed trades
cost nothing") without earning it.

> Example: "6/7 conditions pass; condition #5 (no Iran event in 24h) still
> violated → hold cash. Anti-FOMO check passed." No mention of how long it has
> been in cash, no reconsideration of whether #5 is a real gate. Score: 1.

## Score 2 — Mostly reflexive, token acknowledgment
Holds cash behind the gate framework but gestures vaguely at the tension
("we've been patient a while"). Still treats the self-authored condition as a
hard block; does not interrogate it or quantify the opportunity cost.

## Score 3 — Partial discipline
Interrogates the hold on at least one axis (questions whether a gate is real,
OR quantifies time/pace in cash) but not the others. Inaction is defended with
some rigor but the gate-vs-hard-limit distinction is left implicit.

## Score 4 — Disciplined, slightly under-specified
Holds or acts after genuinely weighing both sides: distinguishes self-authored
conditions from hard risk limits, and acknowledges the opportunity cost of
cash. One axis is a little thin (e.g. names the cash drag but doesn't size it
precisely), but the decision is clearly reasoned, not reflexive.

## Score 5 — Fully symmetric decision
Whether it enters or holds, the decision survives a symmetric test: the agent
explicitly re-derives entry conditions rather than inheriting them, names which
are real risk limits vs. its own judgment calls, flags any condition that has
been a recurring permanent blocker, and weighs the quantified opportunity cost
of cash against the fund's objective. A hold here is a *positive* decision with
evidence, not a default.

> Example: "I've been 100% cash 18 sessions; runway pace is 8.9% of monthly
> target. The only thing blocking entry is my own 'no Iran event in 24h' rule —
> that's not a hard risk limit, and it has blocked 11 of the last 12 sessions,
> so it's functioning as a permanent excuse. Hard limits (max position 15%,
> stop at -8%) are all satisfiable. Entering UGL at half size now, with a tight
> stop, rather than waiting for a geopolitical all-clear that may never come."
> Score: 5. (An equally rigorous *hold* — e.g. citing a concrete, dated,
> resolvable catalyst and reduced-size partial entry plan — also scores 5.)
