# Rubric: restraint (1-5)

Score whether the agent avoids writing generic platitudes — vague lessons that
could apply to any fund, in any market, at any time, without any specific data.

Examples of platitudes (penalize heavily):
- "Always manage risk carefully."
- "Stick to the plan."
- "Be careful with position sizing."
- "Follow your stop-loss rules."
- "Don't let emotions drive decisions."
- "Diversification reduces risk."

A genuine lesson ties a specific action or observation to a specific outcome,
with concrete data from the source material.

## Score 1 — Only platitudes
All or most written lessons are generic advice with no specific data. Nothing
that couldn't have been written without reading the source handoffs or journal.

> Example: "Risk management is important. Always size positions carefully.
> Use stops to protect capital." → three platitudes, no data. Score: 1.

## Score 2 — Mostly platitudes
More than half the lessons are platitudes. A few data points appear but they
are attached to generic advice rather than specific findings.

## Score 3 — Mixed
Roughly half the lessons are genuine and half are platitudes. The genuine
ones have data; the platitudes are brief and don't overwhelm the file.

## Score 4 — Mostly genuine
At most one platitude. The majority of entries are specific and data-backed.
The one platitude does not dominate the file.

## Score 5 — No platitudes
Every lesson is specific, data-backed, and non-obvious. Generic advice is
absent. If the agent had nothing genuine to add, it wrote nothing (which is
the correct behavior when no new lesson is worth recording).

> Example of Score 5 lesson: "2026-05-04 — Transition regime (score 2.1)
> favors cyclicals over defensives: PG entry as defensive ballast lost -3.03%
> while MU gained +10.28% on the same day, confirming the regime-strategy
> mismatch cost real capital." → specific, data-backed, non-obvious. Score: 5.
