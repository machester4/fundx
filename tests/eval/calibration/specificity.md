# Rubric: specificity (1-5)

Score whether each lesson written to the memory files cites concrete, specific
data from the source handoffs or journal entries — prices, dates, percentages,
indicator values, ticker symbols — rather than vague generalities.

## Score 1 — No specifics
Lessons contain no concrete data. Pure generalizations ("market conditions
changed", "the trade worked out"). Could have been written without reading
any source material.

> Example: "Regime changes affect position sizing." → no regime score, date,
> or position size cited. Score: 1.

## Score 2 — Mostly vague
One or two data points appear but most lessons lack specificity. Numbers
present but not tied to the actual events in the source material.

> Example: "The CPI print in May was higher than expected, so we reduced
> exposure." → no specific CPI figure, no reduction size, no date. Score: 2.

## Score 3 — Partially specific
About half the lessons cite concrete data. The actionable lesson is present
but supporting evidence is thin or approximate.

> Example: "CPI at 3.2% (vs 3.0% expected) triggered a risk-budget trim in
> May." → CPI figures cited but no trim size or date. Score: 3.

## Score 4 — Mostly specific
Most lessons cite two or more concrete data points. Only minor gaps (e.g.,
a date implied but not stated, or a percentage rounded).

> Example: "2026-05-01 CPI beat (3.2% vs 3.0%) led to reducing NVDA from
> 12% to 8% weight to free risk budget." → date, ticker, specific weights all
> present. Score: 4.

## Score 5 — Fully specific
Every lesson references concrete data traceable to the source handoffs or
journal: ticker symbols, dates, prices, pnl percentages, or indicator scores.
Nothing could have been written without reading the source material.

> Example: "2026-05-06 — PG cut at -3.03% loss after mid-session review
> showed Transition regime (score 2.1) favors cyclicals, not defensives;
> redeployed half to MU which closed at +12.4% on 2026-05-08." → all figures
> match source. Score: 5.
